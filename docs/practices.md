# From lessons to practices

Homeward distinguishes understanding an idea from trying a concrete action. Practices have no prerequisite lesson: the short guide contains enough to try one small action. The first two are Prep one meal ahead and Check in over dinner.

Each plan has a user-authored or edited action and a cue, phrased When / I’ll. The cue may be an event or a specific time. It is a personal plan, not a scheduled notification. Next and Save & leave explicitly save each step. The ready state keeps instructions available while the person tries the action away from the screen. A later self-report can be Worked, Tried but adjust, or Did not get to it, with an optional note. Repeat retains prior snapshots and starts another plan with the previous wording available to edit.

Practice progress lives under `learning.activities` in the existing private learning JSON; no schema migration is needed. Version checks and operation IDs prevent stale writes and duplicate completions. Twenty XP are awarded only on the first recorded attempt of each practice, including an imperfect try. Planning and Did not get to it do not earn XP. Practice XP joins the learning dashboard total; older dinner-practice XP remains separate. XP is participation credit, not proof of skill or habit formation.

## Content basis

- Creator-supplied Practical Home Systems Workbook, pp. 41–44, especially page 44: look at the real week, prepare one or two days ahead, and work with familiar meal components. The app starts with one prep task for one meal; it does not reproduce affiliate links or promise an entire weekly system.
- [Gottman: How to Have a Stress-Reducing Conversation](https://www.gottman.com/blog/how-to-stress-reducing-conversation/), checked 25 October 2026: turn-taking, listening about external stresses, reflecting understanding and avoiding unsolicited advice. The five-minute dinner invitation is Homeward’s adaptation, not a validated Gottman protocol or assessment.
- [USDA: poultry marinating](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/poultry-basting-brining-and-marinating) and [FoodSafety.gov: four food-safety steps](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety), checked 25 October 2026, support the practical storage guidance. Preparing or marinating does not reset a food’s storage clock.

The cue/action format is a product mechanism for planning one try. This release does not claim a validated habit intervention, automatic reminders, mastery, or a fixed number of days to form a habit.
